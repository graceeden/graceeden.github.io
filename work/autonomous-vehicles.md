<section class="page"><div class="wrap"><div class="head"><div class="label">Human–autonomous systems</div><div><h1>Human–AV Interaction Design</h1><p class="intro">Research into how pedestrians can understand, interpret and negotiate interactions with autonomous vehicles.</p></div></div><div class="two"><div class="panel"><h3>Research question</h3><p>How can interactions between pedestrians and autonomous vehicles communicate what an autonomous vehicle is doing and intends to do?</p></div><div class="panel"><h3>Approach</h3><p>Empirical HCI, field research and participatory interaction design, grounded in the perspectives and practices of road users.</p></div></div><div style="max-width:850px;margin-top:50px"><h2>Why it matters</h2><p>Autonomous vehicles change the familiar assumptions people make about eye contact, driver intention and vehicle behaviour. My work examined these interactions from the perspective of pedestrians rather than treating the vehicle as the sole centre of the design problem.</p><div class="quote">Designing autonomous mobility means designing communication between people and systems, not simply automating the vehicle.</div><h2>Research contribution</h2><p>This programme contributed to HCI research on communication, transparency and experience around autonomous vehicles, including CHI publications on pedestrian interaction and autonomous passenger shuttles.</p></div></div></section>
